# FogSift Channel Plan: Teach First, Get Paid Anyway

**Date:** 2026-10-01
**Companion to:** `FOGSIFT_BUSINESS_BRIEF.md` (what exists, what the pieces are). This document is the operating plan for the YouTube channel as the primary revenue vessel.

---

## 1. The reframe that makes this work

You have a teacher's heart and you hate sales. Good. That is not the obstacle. It is the strategy.

**The viewer never pays. The vendor pays.** When someone learns how to do a real task with a tool and then buys it through the link under the lesson, the software company pays FogSift a commission. The student paid tuition of zero. You taught. The vendor covered it. That is the entire business model, and it does not require you to persuade anyone of anything. It requires you to be useful on camera and put the link where they can find it.

This is also what the numbers say. Tutorial content performs about **5x better than promotional content** for affiliate results, and how-to videos keep pulling search traffic for months or years after publication, so the commissions compound with lifetime views instead of spiking and dying like a pitch. A salesman makes a video that works for a week. A teacher makes one that works for three years.

So the rule for every video: **teach the outcome, mention the tool, link it, disclose it, move on.** You never have to say "you should buy this." You are allowed to say "don't buy this if." Saying it is what makes the links under the other videos convert.

## 2. Why YouTube specifically, and what it needs from you

YouTube rewards exactly two things: watch time and return visits. Tutorials deliver both, because people watch to the end to finish the task and come back when they have the next one.

The milestones, in order:
- **500 subscribers + 3,000 watch hours** unlocks fan funding, memberships, and (as of March 2026) **YouTube Shopping affiliate tagging** directly in videos and Shorts.
- **1,000 subscribers + 4,000 watch hours** unlocks ads. New applicants accepted after **Feb 1, 2027 need 8,000 hours**, so the push is to get in before then.
- Sponsorships do not wait for either. Small tech channels under 10k subscribers are getting **$50 to $250 per integration and $100 to $500 per dedicated video** in 2026. Software and B2B-adjacent content sits in the highest CPM bracket. A hybrid deal (small flat fee plus 20 to 30% affiliate) is the normal ask at this size.

None of that lowers the bar for the work. It just means the work pays on more than one channel once it exists.

## 3. Pick a lane: curriculum, not catalog

A salesman reviews whatever is trending. A teacher runs a syllabus. The syllabus is what turns a pile of videos into a channel people subscribe to, and later into a product you can sell outright.

**The lane:** *Using AI and automation tools to get real work done, for people who are not developers.*

Why this lane and not another:
- It is what you already know and already teach. "How to Talk to AI" exists as a PDF. The AI Learning Lab installers exist. The homepage bio already says "teaching faceless YouTube monetization."
- The affiliate programs in this lane are the best-paying recurring ones available (Notion 50%, Make 35%, Descript 30%, ElevenLabs 22%, Canva $36 flat).
- The audience is huge, confused, and underserved by people who can explain things plainly. The fog is thick here. Sifting it is the brand.
- It feeds Johnny Autoseed later. Automation for non-developers is the same lesson whether the thing being automated is a spreadsheet or a FarmBot.

**Season 1 syllabus (12 lessons, one playlist, one course):**

| 2 | How to turn a messy folder of notes into a searchable second brain | Notion + AI | Notion (PartnerStack) |
| 3 | How to make a 10-minute video sound like you had a studio | Descript | Descript |
| 4 | How to automate the one task you do every single morning | Make.com | Make |

Lesson 8 has no affiliate link on purpose. One lesson in twelve that cannot possibly make you money is what makes the other eleven believable.

## 4. The three video shapes

Everything the channel publishes is one of these. Nothing else.

**Lesson** (8 to 15 minutes). One outcome, one tool, one real task done start to finish on screen. Face in corner. Structure: what we are making (10 seconds), disclosure (10 seconds), do the thing, the "don't bother if" clause, the one-sentence ask. This is the backbone and where the affiliate money lives.

**Sift** (5 to 8 minutes). "A vs B for [job]" or "Is X worth it for [person]." A verdict, said plainly, with the reasons. Links to both. Lower volume, higher conversion, because people watching a comparison are about to buy something.

**Field note** (Short, under 60 seconds). One trick, cut from a lesson. Zero extra recording. These exist to pull subscribers toward the 500 mark and to surface the lessons.

**Cadence that is sustainable for one person:** 2 lessons + 1 sift per week, plus 3 field notes cut from them. One recording day per week. That is roughly 24 to 30 minutes of long-form per week, which is enough to be a channel and not enough to burn out.

## 5. The teacher's rules for selling without being a salesman

Print these. Put them next to the camera.

1. **Teach the outcome, not the tool.** The title is "How to [result]," never "[Tool] review."
2. **Disclosure is the first thing after the hook.** "FogSift earns a commission if you buy through the links below. That is how this channel pays for itself." Same sentence every time. First line of the description too. Paid-promotion toggle on.
3. **Every lesson has a "don't bother if" clause.** Who should not buy this. Said out loud.
4. **Recommend the free option when it exists.** This costs you a few commissions and buys you all the trust.
5. **No countdowns, no scarcity, no "limited time," no hype voice.** If the deal expires, say the date like a weather report.
6. **One ask, one sentence, at the end, always the same.** "If this helped, the links are below, and if you want us to sift something for you, that's twenty bucks, also below."
7. **Never say "you should buy this."** You are allowed to say "this is what I use" and "this one is not worth it."
8. **The verdict is allowed to be no.** A channel whose verdicts are always yes is a catalog. A channel that sometimes says no is a teacher.

## 7. The weekly operating rhythm

| Day | Block | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Research + script | Pick the week's 2 lessons + 1 sift from the syllabus. Write the outcome, the steps, the "don't bother if," the ask. Half-page per video, not a script. |
| Tue | Record | All three in one sitting. Screen recording + face cam. No editing today. |
| Wed | Edit | Lessons. Cut dead air, add the disclosure card, chapter markers. |
| Thu | Edit + cut | The sift, plus 3 field notes cut from the lessons. Thumbnails. Descriptions from the template. |
| Fri | Publish + queue | Publish one. Schedule the other two for Sun and Wed. Work the $20 queue. Update the scoreboard. |
| Sat/Sun | Off | Life, health, sanity. Your terms page already says so. |

**The scoreboard** (one sheet, updated Friday, six numbers): videos published, watch hours (28-day), subscribers, affiliate clicks, commissions tracked, $20 submissions. Nothing else. If a number has not moved in three weeks, that is the thing to fix.

## 8. The first fourteen days

**Days 1 to 3** (already planned in the brief): apply to PartnerStack, Impact and the direct programs. Write the disclosure once. Record three lessons from tools you already use daily. Publish one per day starting day 3.

**Days 4 to 7:**
- Make the "How to Talk to AI" PDF free for email. Put the signup link in every description.
- Reframe the $20 offer on the site as "Should we use this?" and make all eight pages agree.
- Add a "How FogSift makes money" page. Plain words, one screen. Link it in every description. This is the brand line as a page, and the FTC will want it regardless.
- Record lessons 4 to 6.

**Days 8 to 14:**
- Publish on the Fri / Sun / Wed rhythm.
- Cut the first field notes.
- Email 10 people you know: "I'm teaching this on YouTube now. If you have a tool or decision you want sifted, it's $20 and it becomes a lesson." Those are the first real queue entries and the first real testimonials.
- Trim the homepage and about bio to a presenter's bio. You stay named; the life story leaves the sales pages.
- First scoreboard entry.

By day 14: six lessons live, links under every one, a disclosure page, an email list with a reason to join, the $20 offer consistent everywhere, and the first real queue submissions. That is a channel with a business attached, two weeks in.

## 9. What FogSift (the site) does in this plan

The site stops trying to be a consulting firm's brochure and becomes the channel's back office:

- **Homepage:** Watch (primary), the $20 "Should we use this?" (secondary), the free PDF (email capture). Nothing else above the fold.
- **How we make money page:** disclosure, in your voice, one screen.
- **Lessons page:** the Season 1 playlist embedded, with the links and the "don't bother if" notes under each. This becomes the course page later.
- **Queue:** real "should we use this" submissions, public by default.
- **Offers:** $20, $500, contracted. Unchanged, less prominent.
- Everything else (wiki, vision, shouse, engine, lore) stays reachable from the footer and out of the way.

## 10. One thing to keep in view

You said you do not need anyone to like you. Fine. The format does not require that. It requires you to be useful for eight minutes at a time, which is a thing you already do by instinct. The teacher's heart is not something you have to put away to make money here. It is the only part of you the channel needs, and the part that makes the links convert. The rest of you gets to stay home.
